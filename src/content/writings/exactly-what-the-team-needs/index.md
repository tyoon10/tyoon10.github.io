You wrote that you are hiring a Google Workspace product marketing manager for the rare person fluent in both product roadmaps and human sentences, and to slide into your DMs asking for a friend. I am the friend. Here is why I am also the hire.

That line is a joke because the two fluencies almost never live in one person. Marketers who can write the human sentence usually cannot read the roadmap, so they wait for a brief and repeat it. The PMs and engineers who can read the roadmap usually cannot write the sentence, so the value dies in the gap between the capability and the customer. Workspace is an AI platform now: Gemini across Gmail, Docs, and Sheets, NotebookLM standing on its own, and a business decision maker who still has to be told why any of it changes their quarter. That needs both fluencies at once. Here is my evidence for each.

## I can read the roadmap

I do not wait for the brief; I read the system. I write technical deep-dives on the layer underneath the product: [why agents need better infrastructure than better models](/writings/agents-need-better-infrastructure/), [what memory should look like for a coding agent](/writings/files-are-the-memory/), and [what held across 13 months of Claude Code's source](/writings/tracing-the-minds-behind-claude-code/). I led the machine learning behind an AI company I co-founded and sold, and I still ship tooling on GitHub. As a product manager, roadmaps are where I live.

**What that buys you:** when the next Gemini-in-Workspace capability lands, I already know which one is the story and which one is a footnote, and I can tell them apart before the launch, not after.

## I can write the human sentence

Then I turn the system into a sentence people act on. My most-read piece argues that [context engineering, not prompt engineering, is the skill that separates AI-native product managers from everyone else](/writings/ai-superpower-for-pm/); reframing a category like that is the whole job of positioning. I have run 40+ sessions translating AI for rooms that ran from undergrads to CTOs, holding one message steady across every one of them. That is narrative architecture: pick the single true sentence to lead with, then keep every channel saying it.

**What that buys you:** a portfolio voice that does not fracture across web, social, sales decks, and the stage, because one person is deciding which sentence leads and resolving the conflicts when they collide.

## I can do both for a business decision maker

The role is built around the BDM and outcome-based selling, and that is the only way I have ever sold. I have stood in front of that audience: I guest-lectured [Directors and C-suite leaders on AI](/writings/what-senior-business-leaders-ask-about-ai/), so I know a BDM asks about liability, talent, and competitive advantage, not a feature list. I ran a [12-week product strategy engagement with CNN](/projects/access-to-experts/), diagnosing a business problem and designing a positioned solution their senior leadership signed off on. The company I sold was measured in retention and conversion, not feature counts. And I ship go-to-market end to end: I launched [claude-ensemble](https://github.com/tyoon10/claude-ensemble) on the back of blind, length-controlled A/B evals, because a claim a BDM can act on has to be true, not loud. A CFA charter means I speak their language of risk and return without a translator.

**What that buys you:** mentoring a sales team from feature-based to outcome-based selling is not a skill I would learn on your budget; it is the translation I already do by reflex.

## The rare combination

So I read the roadmap, I write the sentence, and I do both for the person who signs. Most of my public work happens to sit in one AI ecosystem, but the skill is portable, and the story is the one Workspace is already telling with Gemini: **AI is a multiplier on work people already do, not a feature bolted onto it.**

You wrote your post the way you did because this combination is genuinely hard to hire. I already know which Workspace story I would tell first, and which one I would kill. Let's talk about whether I'm right.

Asking for a friend.
