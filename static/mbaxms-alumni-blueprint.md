# MBAxMS Alumni Association Blueprint

## executive summary

The Columbia MBAxMS program — a dual MBA/MS degree spanning Columbia Business School and the School of Engineering — launched its first cohort of ~26 students in Fall 2023 (Class of 2025). Now entering its third year with a Class of 2027 cohort of ~47, the program is growing fast but lacks the alumni infrastructure to match. Dean Shih-Fu Chang (Columbia Engineering) has asked Hugh Thomas (Co-Director, MBAxMS) to formalize an Alumni Association. This blueprint proposes the structure, programming, and AI-powered engagement strategy for that association, with the goal of becoming a model for how modern alumni communities operate.

The core thesis: **the MBAxMS alumni association should not replicate the traditional alumni association playbook.** The program's small size, dual-school identity, and technology-fluent membership create an opportunity to build something fundamentally different — a tight-knit, AI-augmented community that treats alumni engagement as a product, not an obligation.

## problem statement

### what students are saying

> "Dual degree students are treated like the red-headed stepchild. Both schools keep passing the buck when it comes to program requirements and restrictions."

> "Double the responsibility, higher requirements, higher tuition; but the value isn't clear enough. It feels like the two separate programs have been welded together, without considerate integration of uniquely dual degree experience."

> "While we have a strong sense of solidarity among our cohort, we feel largely disconnected with other cohorts. We want to know the previous class' lived experience, but no one's heard of what they are doing after they graduated."

> "Some of the staffs are never responsive. They are unreachable whenever in most need. It feels we're never their priority."

> "We really want a cool merch for the program, that is uniquely representative of the dual degree program of CBS and SEAS."

### root causes

1. **Institutional orphan problem** — Neither CBS nor SEAS fully "owns" MBAxMS students, resulting in responsibility gaps and deprioritization by both schools' administrative staff
2. **No inter-cohort connective tissue** — Each cohort bonds internally but has zero structured interaction with past or future cohorts; once a class graduates, the connection to the program evaporates
3. **Invisible alumni outcomes** — Current students cannot see what career paths graduates have taken, eroding confidence in the program's ROI
4. **No dedicated advocacy channel** — Student concerns about the program have no formal path to reach leadership at either school

### what the alumni association solves

The association creates a **persistent identity layer** for MBAxMS — one that outlasts the 2-year program window, connects cohorts vertically, gives graduates a reason to stay engaged, and gives the program a feedback loop that improves over time.

## vision and principles

### mission

To build and sustain the MBAxMS community beyond graduation — connecting alumni, current students, faculty, and staff through meaningful engagement that reinforces the unique value of the dual-degree experience.

### vision

The most connected small-program alumni network at Columbia — a model for how modern, technology-fluent alumni communities operate.

### guiding principles

1. **Belonging over branding** — The association exists to make people feel connected, not to produce marketing collateral. Every initiative should pass the test: "does this make someone feel more part of this community?"

2. **Small and intentional** — The program's small size is a feature, not a bug. Intimacy enables trust. Design for 150-person Dunbar-number dynamics, not 10,000-person broadcast dynamics.

3. **Bridge, don't silo** — The whole point of MBAxMS is spanning two worlds. The association should embody that: events that mix cohorts, programming that bridges business and tech, leadership that includes both schools.

4. **Vertical connection** — The highest-value engagement is between cohorts. Class of 2025 alumni mentoring Class of 2027 students. Class of 2026 alumni co-hosting events with Class of 2028 students. Every program should have a vertical axis.

5. **AI-native operations** — This community is built by and for people who understand AI. The association should use AI not as a gimmick but as core infrastructure — for event coordination, interest matching, and reducing the operational burden on volunteers.

6. **Low friction, high signal** — Minimize the number of platforms, logins, and obligations. Maximize the ratio of valuable interactions to noise.

## membership model

### who is a member

All MBAxMS degree holders and current students are automatic members. No dues, no application. The community is defined by the shared experience of the dual degree.

### membership tiers

| Tier | Who | Access |
|---|---|---|
| **Alumni Members** | Every MBAxMS graduate | Directory, annual Homecoming, newsletter, Slack/WhatsApp group (combined / cohort) |
| **Student Members** | Current MBAxMS student (two classes) | Directory, annual Homecoming, newsletter, Slack/WhatsApp group |
| **Class Representatives** | 1-2 per cohort, elected or volunteered | Serve as cohort liaison; organize class-specific events |
| **Leadership** | President + committee leads | Set strategy, manage budget, interface with program co-directors and deans |

There are no dues to join the membership.

- Designs and runs mentorship matching (alumni ↔ current students)

### class representatives

Each cohort elects or appoints 1-2 class representatives who:
- Serve as the primary communication channel between the association and their cohort
- Organize 1-2 class-specific events per year
- Provide cohort-level input to leadership on programming and priorities
- Participate in the semi-annual advisory council meeting

**Current student representatives** (1-2 per enrolled cohort) serve as the bridge between active students and the alumni body. They attend association meetings, relay student concerns, and help coordinate mentor matching.

### advisory council

Meets twice per year (Fall and Spring). Composed of:
- Association leadership (President + VPs)
- All class representatives
- Program Co-Directors (Hugh Thomas + counterpart)
- Dedicated staff liaison (see below)
- 1 representative from CBS Development & Alumni Relations
- 1 representative from SEAS Alumni Relations

### institutional support

A **dedicated alumni liaison** from the MBAxMS program staff should be designated. This person:
- Serves as the day-to-day operational contact between the association and the university
- Manages the alumni directory and contact database
- Coordinates with CBS DAR and SEAS alumni offices on logistics (venue booking, email sends, etc.)
- Ensures alumni events appear on both schools' calendars

This role directly addresses the student complaint about unresponsive staff. A named, accountable liaison creates a single point of contact.

## programming: the event series

### Tier 1: Signature Annual Events

#### Homecoming Day
- **Who**: All alumni + current students + faculty + staff
- **When**: Annual, fall semester (ideally during a CBS or SEAS reunion weekend to leverage existing campus energy)
- **Format**: Reception at Geffen Board Room or comparable venue
- **Program**: State-of-the-program update from Co-Directors → alumni lightning talks (3-5 min each, "what I'm working on now") → open networking with food and drinks
- **Goal**: The one event everyone comes back for. Anchor it to the calendar early and make it the can't-miss gathering.
- **Budget**: Covered by program/school sponsorship; flagship event justifies institutional investment

#### Capstone Demo Day
- **Who**: Second-year presenters + first-year MBAxMS students + alumni + faculty + staff. Alumni are encouraged to attend to reconnect with current and former students, faculty, and the program community.
- **When**: Annual, early May on campus (existing graduation requirement for second-year students)
- **Format**: Industry professional-level showcase of engineering builds, startup ventures, and applied AI work — positioned as a professional demo, not a class project presentation
- **Program**: Capstone presentations → live demos / poster session → networking reception with alumni and faculty
- **Goal**: Elevate the capstone demo from an academic requirement into a signature program event. By opening it to alumni, it becomes a recurring touchpoint that draws graduates back to campus, makes student work visible to the broader network, and creates a flywheel where alumni return to see the next cohort's work, offer feedback, and recruit from the pipeline.

### Tier 2: Recurring Community Events

#### Class Day
- **Who**: Respective cohort only (e.g., all Class of 2026 members)
- **When**: Flexible cadence — at least once per year, more if the class rep organizes it
- **Format**: Dinner, activity, or casual gathering; class rep decides based on cohort preferences
- **AI integration**: The AI event coordinator (see AI Strategy below) suggests themes and venues based on class member preferences and history
- **Budget**: Partially covered by program/school sponsorship (once a year)
- **Goal**: Maintain the intra-cohort bonds that are the program's strongest social asset

#### Cross-Cohort Mixer
- **Who**: 2 adjacent cohorts (e.g., Class of 2025 + Class of 2026)
- **When**: 1-2x per year
- **Format**: Casual venue — bar, restaurant, activity
- **Goal**: Build the vertical connections between cohorts that don't currently exist. This is the highest-priority gap to close.

#### Regional Hub Events
- **Who**: Alumni in a specific metro area
- **When**: Quarterly in NYC (largest concentration); 1-2x/year in SF/Bay Area; ad hoc elsewhere
- **Format**: Dinners, happy hours, or co-working days
- **AI integration**: AI coordinator identifies clusters of alumni in the same city and suggests gatherings when a critical mass exists

### Tier 3: Career & Learning

#### Mentor Matching Program
- **Who**: Alumni mentors + current student mentees
- **When**: Matched annually (fall), with quarterly check-in prompts
- **Format**: 1-on-1 mentorship pairs, matched on career interest, industry, and personality
- **AI integration**: AI-powered matching based on profiles, interests, and career goals

#### Career Panels & Fireside Chats
- **Who**: Open to all members
- **When**: 2-3x per year (virtual to maximize attendance)
- **Format**: 2-3 alumni panelists in a shared industry or role type + moderated Q&A
- **Topics**: "MBAxMS in finance," "Building in AI post-graduation," "Navigating the job search as a dual-degree grad"

#### Alumni Office Hours
- **Who**: Alumni volunteers + current students
- **When**: Monthly, virtual, rotating alumni hosts
- **Format**: Drop-in 30-min slots for career advice, resume review, or general conversation

### Tier 4: Lightweight Engagement

#### MBAxMS Swag
- **Who**: All members
- **When**: Distribution at Homecoming and graduation; online store for on-demand orders
- **Items**: Co-branded CBS × SEAS merchandise that reflects the dual identity — something neither school's bookstore sells
- **Goal**: Physical identity artifact. Sounds trivial, but this has been one of the most asked feature - a shared swag that creates visible belonging.

#### Newsletter / Annual Update
- **Who**: All members
- **Format**: Quarterly email newsletter + annual "State of MBAxMS" report
- **Content**: Alumni milestones, upcoming events, program news, career opportunities shared by alumni
- **AI integration**: AI generates draft content from alumni updates and event recaps; VP Communications edits and sends

## ai strategy: the event coordination engine

### the problem with volunteer-run events

The current workflow for any MBAxMS gathering:

1. Someone says: "who wants to join me and Justin to go to this Thai restaurant this Sunday?"
2. The host finds a venue, makes a reservation, creates a Partiful link, collects RSVPs, coordinates payment, manages logistics
3. People attend
4. Host chases down payments after the event

This works when someone is motivated to host. It breaks when no one volunteers — which is most of the time. The result: events happen sporadically, driven by the energy of a few individuals, with no institutional memory of what worked.

### the ai event coordinator

An AI-powered system that reduces the friction of organizing gatherings by handling the operational overhead while keeping humans in the loop for the parts that matter.

#### what the AI does

**1. Interest profiling & matching**
- Maintains a lightweight profile for each member: location, interests, dietary preferences, availability patterns
- Examples from current cohort:
  - Jacq (Class of 2026) is looking for marathon running partners
  - Conor lives in New York
  - Trevor and Winnie ski (recently went to Catskills)
  - Pize lives in NYC and hosts small-group dinner pods at restaurants
- AI identifies interest clusters and location overlaps across cohorts

**2. Event suggestion & demand sensing**
- Based on interest clusters, the AI proposes gathering themes (e.g., "5 MBAxMS alumni in NYC are interested in skiing — want to plan a day trip?")
- Sends lightweight polls to gauge interest before committing to logistics
- Adjusts suggestions based on response patterns (if ski trips get low engagement, suggest fewer; if dinner pods are popular, suggest more)

**3. Logistics automation**
- Once demand is confirmed, the AI handles: venue research → reservation → RSVP link creation → reminders → post-event payment splitting
- Uses existing tools (OpenTable/Resy for restaurants, Partiful or Luma for RSVPs, Splitwise or Venmo Request for payments)
- Human host approves key decisions (final venue choice, budget) but doesn't have to do the grunt work

**4. Event memory & learning**
- Maintains a history of all events: what happened, who attended, satisfaction signals (did people come back for similar events?)
- Uses this history to improve future suggestions
- Identifies members who haven't attended anything in 6+ months and suggests low-commitment re-engagement (e.g., a virtual coffee chat)

#### what the AI does NOT do

- **Replace the human host**: Every event still has a named human host or co-hosts. The AI is the operations layer, not the social layer. People come because a friend invited them, not because a bot did.
- **Make unilateral decisions**: The AI suggests, polls, and coordinates. It doesn't book a venue without approval or send invitations without a host's sign-off.
- **Create social pressure**: No "you haven't attended in a while" guilt messages. Re-engagement prompts should feel like a friend reaching out, not a CRM drip campaign.

#### implementation approach

**Phase 1 (Year 1): Manual with structured data**
- Build the interest/preference database via a simple onboarding form
- Use a shared spreadsheet or Airtable to track events and attendance
- AI assistance is behind-the-scenes: the VP of Programming uses ChatGPT/Claude to draft event proposals, match interests, and generate logistics checklists
- No member-facing AI yet — just smarter human coordination

**Phase 2 (Year 2): Semi-automated coordination**
- Deploy a lightweight bot (Slack bot or WhatsApp integration) that can:
  - Poll members on event interest
  - Send reminders and RSVP links
  - Collect post-event feedback
- AI generates event suggestions based on accumulated data; VP of Programming curates and approves

**Phase 3 (Year 3+): Full event coordination engine**
- Members interact directly with the AI coordinator to propose, discover, and join events
- AI handles end-to-end logistics for standard event types (dinner, activity, virtual chat)
- System learns from multi-year data to predict engagement and optimize programming

### funding strategy

1. **Primary: Program/school sponsorship** — The association is a strategic asset for the MBAxMS program (retention, admissions, reputation). Position the budget ask as an investment in program growth.
2. **Secondary: Event cost-sharing** — Most recurring events (dinners, activities) are paid by attendees. Association subsidizes partially to keep costs accessible.
3. **Future: Corporate sponsorship** — As the alumni network grows, employers hiring from MBAxMS may sponsor events in exchange for access (career panels, networking dinners). This is a Year 2+ play.
4. **No member dues in Year 1** — Prove value first.

## what makes this different

Most alumni associations are built on a broadcast model: the school sends emails, hosts an annual gala, and asks for donations. Engagement is low because the value proposition is abstract ("stay connected to your alma mater") and the interaction model is one-to-many.

The MBAxMS Alumni Association is designed differently because it can be:

1. **Small enough to be personal** — With ~100 total alumni by 2027, every member can know every other member. This is a dinner party, not a stadium.

2. **Dual-identity as a feature** — Spanning CBS and SEAS gives the association a unique position: it's the only alumni group at Columbia that inherently bridges business and engineering. This makes it valuable to both schools' alumni offices and to employers looking for that rare combination.

3. **AI-native from day one** — Not AI as a buzzword, but AI as operational infrastructure. The members understand and expect this. The program's identity is literally at the intersection of business and technology. An alumni association that doesn't use AI to coordinate itself would be ironic.

4. **Designed for the post-graduation relationship** — Traditional associations focus on what alumni can give back (donations, mentorship, brand advocacy). This association focuses first on what alumni get (connection, career support, belonging) — trusting that giving back follows naturally when people feel genuinely connected.

## appendix: founder's note

The MBAxMS program changed my trajectory. It gave me a cohort of people who think the way I do — across business and technology, refusing to be boxed into one or the other. But the program is young, and the infrastructure hasn't caught up to the community's potential.

This blueprint is my attempt to build that infrastructure before the window closes. The first few cohorts will define what this community becomes. If we get it right — if graduating from MBAxMS means joining a tight, active, mutually supportive network — then the program's value compounds with every class. If we don't, each cohort will scatter after graduation, and the dual-degree experience will remain, as one student put it, "two separate programs welded together."

I don't think that's inevitable. I think we can build something better.
